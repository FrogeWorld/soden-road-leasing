Planning & uses

Find the right fit
for the Green Wedge.

The Victorian Government’s property report identifies this address as Green Wedge Zone Schedule 1 (GWZ1), with Vegetation Protection Overlay Schedule 1 (VPO1). The exact activity and any existing approvals still need to be checked with the City of Greater Dandenong.

!

A permit is not a workaround for a prohibited use. Buildings, works and signs may trigger separate approvals even where the land use needs no permit. VPO1 also means any proposed vegetation removal or lopping should be checked with Council before work starts.

Generally no use permit*

Agriculture & grazing

Many forms of agriculture and grazing animal production sit in the zone’s permit-not-required section. Horticulture or growing crops may be a natural fit for the land.

*Only where the exact use meets Section 1 conditions. Buildings, works and overlays need separate checks.
Planning permit required

Rural enterprises

Examples in the zone’s permit-required section include plant nursery, rural industry, domestic animal boarding, agricultural produce cool storage, solid fuel depot and vehicle store.

“Rural industry” has a specific planning definition, including processing agricultural produce or servicing agricultural plant.
Generally prohibited

Conventional urban uses

Ordinary offices, most general warehousing, most general industry and most retail premises are in the prohibited section of the Green Wedge Zone.

Do not assume a shed can become a general warehouse or workshop simply because the building exists.

Have a different idea? Describe the actual activity, materials, vehicles, staff and hours to Council. The planning definition matters more than the name of your business.

The approval pathway

From idea
to permitted use.

For uses that need permission, the City of Greater Dandenong is the place to confirm the requirements and assess an application. Approval is assessed on the proposal and is never automatic.

Council pre-application service ↗
  1. 01

    Define your operation

    Set out the proposed use, area needed, site layout, hours, staffing, traffic and vehicle types, equipment, materials and any changes to sheds or land.

  2. 02

    Check site controls

    Review the current property report: it shows GWZ1 and VPO1. Check title restrictions and request any existing planning permits or endorsed plans relevant to the space.

  3. 03

    Speak with Council

    Ask Council for advice or use its pre-application discussion service. Council lists all Green Wedge Zone applications as complex for this service.

  4. 04

    Apply if needed

    Submit a complete planning permit application with current title documents, plans and supporting information. Council may request more detail, refer or advertise the proposal, and impose conditions if approved.

  5. 05

    Secure all approvals before use

    Check whether building, health, environmental, access or other approvals also apply. Comply with any planning permit and endorsed plans before commencing the approved use.

Before you enquire

Good questions.
Clear next steps.

Can I lease just one shed or part of the land?

Discuss the area you need with Tina. Available spaces, boundaries and lease terms should be confirmed during inspection.

Are mains services available?

The property has mains water and electricity. Confirm the connection, metering and capacity for the specific area you want to lease.

Does my business need a planning permit?

It depends on the legally defined land use, existing approvals, the Green Wedge Zone conditions and any overlays. The guide above is a starting point; Council can confirm requirements for your specific proposal.

Can I use a shed for general storage or a workshop?

Do not assume so. Most general warehousing and general industry are prohibited in the Green Wedge Zone. Some narrowly defined rural storage or rural industry uses may be considered under different rules.

Who arranges planning approval?

That should be agreed with Tina as part of lease discussions. A prospective tenant should explain their proposal to Council early and allow for any required approvals before starting the use.
